AQUAGUARD AI — WATER LEAKAGE AND ABNORMAL-USAGE INDICATORS

Purpose:
This document provides general indicators that AquaGuard AI can use when interpreting persistent or unusual water-consumption patterns. These indicators support risk assessment and do not by themselves prove that a physical leak exists.

1. Sudden Increase in Water Consumption

A significant increase above the expected or historical water-consumption pattern may indicate abnormal water use.

Possible causes include:
- Increased water demand
- Continuous water flow
- Plumbing problems
- Equipment malfunction
- Leakage
- Changes in household or facility activity

AquaGuard should compare current consumption with the expected baseline before classifying the event.

2. Persistent Abnormal Consumption

Repeated abnormal consumption over several consecutive observations is more significant than a single isolated increase.

Persistent abnormal usage may indicate:
- Continuous water flow
- Leakage
- Equipment problems
- Unusual operational activity

The number of consecutive anomalies should therefore be considered when assessing risk.

3. Unexpected Low-Demand or Night-Time Consumption

Water consumption during periods when water use is normally low can indicate continuous flow or possible leakage.

Examples include:
- Water consumption during sleeping hours
- Continuous meter movement
- Repeated consumption during facility closure
- Consumption when no major water activity is expected

However, scheduled activities and automated systems should be considered before concluding that leakage is present.

4. Water Meter Movement

If all known water-consuming activities are stopped and the water meter continues to register flow, there may be an unintended source of consumption.

Possible causes include:
- Leaking taps
- Running toilets
- Pipe leakage
- Faulty valves
- Storage tank overflow
- Connected equipment

The observation should be verified before reporting a confirmed leak.

5. Visible Signs of Leakage

Physical indicators may include:
- Dripping taps
- Continuously running toilets
- Wet floors or walls
- Damp areas near pipes
- Water pooling
- Overflowing storage tanks
- Unusual sounds from plumbing
- Moisture around pipe connections

These physical observations provide additional evidence but should not be assumed when they have not been observed.

6. High Consumption from a Specific Source

If monitoring data identifies a particular area, appliance, fixture, or activity as the major contributor to consumption, that source should be investigated first.

Examples include:
- Bathroom
- Kitchen
- Irrigation system
- Water tank
- Industrial equipment
- Washing equipment

7. Importance of Historical Comparison

Current consumption should be compared with:
- Historical consumption
- Expected consumption
- Recent average consumption
- Similar time periods
- Normal operating conditions

A single unusual value may have a legitimate explanation, while a persistent deviation can provide stronger evidence for investigation.

8. AquaGuard Interpretation Rules

Normal:
Consumption is within the expected range. No immediate leakage conclusion should be made.

Warning:
Consumption is moderately above the expected pattern. Continue monitoring and review recent water usage.

High:
Consumption is significantly above the expected pattern. Investigate major contributing sources.

Critical:
Consumption is substantially above the expected pattern, particularly when the anomaly persists. Prompt investigation is recommended.

9. Responsible AI Guidance

AquaGuard must distinguish between:
- Observed measurement
- Statistical anomaly
- Possible cause
- Confirmed physical problem

An abnormal consumption pattern should be described as an indicator of possible leakage rather than proof of leakage.

Preferred language:
- "possible leakage"
- "may indicate"
- "could be caused by"
- "requires investigation"

Avoid:
- "A leak definitely exists."
- "The pipe is definitely broken."
- "The system has confirmed a leak."
